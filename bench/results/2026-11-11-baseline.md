# Performance baseline, 2026-11-11

Base: fork `lab-all` at `59a3b26d` (PR #208 merge), release build, plus the measurement code in this branch
(`OWE_PERF_LOG` stage timers, `OWE_GPU_TIMING` per-pass GPU timestamps, both off by default). Machine: RTX 4070
(driver 595.99), Intel UHD 770 (not used), 30 GB RAM, Linux 7.0. Offscreen rendering with SceneViewer
(`--capture`, no window, unpaced), 40 wallpapers (`wallpapers.json`), 1920x1080 and 5120x1440, shader cache warm.
Raw data: `baseline-1080p.json`, `baseline-5120x1440.json`, `stages-5120x1440/*.json`. Method and caveats: `../README.md`.

## Headline

- **38 of 40 wallpapers already exceed 60 fps at 1080p, and 37 of 40 exceed 120 fps** (uncapped, steady state, 4070).
  Below 60: **More Physics (4.7 fps)** and **Ocarina of Time (26 fps)**. Borderline: LittleBigPlanet (60 fps at 1080p, 56
  at 5120x1440). At 5120x1440 the same 37 stay above 120 fps.
- **The cost is almost entirely CPU, not GPU.** At 5120x1440 (3.6x the pixels) 1080p frame times grow by 0 to 40 %, and
  the GPU time of the busiest scenes is 3 to 3.6 ms (bleach, rain, sculpture-forest, reze). The three slow wallpapers
  are CPU-limited (GPU 0.3 to 8 ms of 14 to 230 ms).
- The viewer renders on the **Intel iGPU by default** on this machine (first device the loader lists): 15 ms for a nebula
  scene that takes 0.8 ms on the 4070. Any tool that runs the viewer without `VK_DRIVER_FILES` (or a UUID) measures the
  wrong GPU. Waywallen passes a UUID, so the desktop path is probably not affected: verify it.

## Ranked improvements (evidence in the sections below)

| # | Area | Affects | Evidence | Potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per-frame frame-graph rebuild in the render thread** (vrento `GraphFrame::begin`, `captureFrame`, `SceneRenderState::update`): bindings re-validated, re-hashed (SipHash), re-allocated every frame though the graph rarely changes | Ocarina (41 ms), LittleBigPlanet (13.6), More Physics (25 ms of its 230), Arknights, Pixels; a floor under every wallpaper | stage timers + perf: Ocarina 22 ms of 41 in begin/capture/update vs 2.6 ms recording | Ocarina 41 -> ~15-20 ms, LBP 13.6 -> ~5; needs a cached/diffed graph, cheap hashes, no per-frame allocations |
| 2 | **JavaScript interpreter** (QuickJS-ng runs scene scripts on the render thread) | More Physics: scripts 177 of 231 ms | perf: 66.6 % `JS_CallInternal`; same cannon-es workload: node 3.1 ms/step, node --jitless 35 ms, QuickJS-ng 51.7 ms | JIT: about 17x on the JS part (231 -> about 60 ms; rank 1 and 4 then dominate); interpreter tuning at most 1.5x |
| 3 | **Full-frame snapshot copies**: `copy` passes of `_rt_default` -> `_rt_default_N_0_copy`, 46 a frame | rain, winter (63 and 81 % of GPU time, 2.2 ms at 5120x1440); copy is the top GPU pass overall (9.1 ms summed over 24 wallpapers) | GPU timestamps by pass and resource | -2 ms GPU on those, less VRAM; blend with fixed function, or share one snapshot per run of layers |
| 4 | **No CPU/GPU overlap** (offscreen path measured; the swapchain path is unverified): one frame in flight, the render thread waits for the fence before the next | every GPU-heavy wallpaper: frame = CPU + GPU (night-city 5.4 ms = 1.4 CPU + 4.0 wait for 2.9 ms of GPU) | `wait` stage vs GPU timestamps | throughput -30 % on GPU-heavy scenes; matters for the 120 fps target; also 0.2-1 ms fence latency above GPU time |
| 5 | **Idle floor**: 6 % of one core at 60 fps for a trivial scene, 57 % of it in one NVIDIA kernel-module function; `submit` costs 0.2-0.27 ms every frame everywhere | all 40 wallpapers (6-12 % of a core at 60 fps even for a nebula) | perf (paced noita), strace, `submit` stage | minimise syncs and ioctls per frame (timeline waits, fewer submits), sleep precisely; unverified cause |
| 6 | **Puppet uniforms**: `PuppetUniformSource::Evaluate` 11 % and `PuppetLayer::genFrame` 5 % of Ocarina's render thread | puppet-heavy scenes | perf | skip static/invisible puppets, cache bone palettes |
| 7 | **Memory**: 465 MB median RSS (317 MB floor), 1.1-1.7 GB for sculpture-forest, night-city, tenna-dance, yingyang-kitty; VRAM up to 1.9 GB (rain at 5120x1440, 0.8 GB at 1080p) | those wallpapers | `--texture-memory-output`: texture CPU buffers peak 363-782 MB but explain only 27-57 % of the RSS | stream textures to the GPU and free them; size render targets by what the layer needs, not the screen; heaptrack needed for the rest |
| 8 | **Effect shaders** by GPU time at 5120x1440: waterwaves 3.6 ms summed over 14 wallpapers, shake 3.4, pulse 3.1, waterflow 2.7, fxaa 1.8 (More Physics), gradient_generator 1.4 | not a problem at 60 or 120 fps today (every one under 1.2 ms) | GPU timestamps | later, for headroom and power |

Smaller findings: the unpaced timer thread spins on a 1 us futex wait (capture mode only); a missing scene package makes
the viewer hang instead of failing; `ms_per_frame` of LittleBigPlanet differs between frame windows (10 vs 16.6 ms) because
its scripts change over time, so compare it only with the same frame counts.

## Where the time goes

**Ocarina of Time** (381 objects, 369 scripts): scripts are 0.5 ms. Of 41 ms: `GraphFrame::begin` 9.5-11 ms
(`GraphBindings::prepare` 18.6 %, `validate_info` 12.6 %, `freeze_info` 4.8 %), `captureFrame` 6-7 ms
(`UniformBufferBinding::Update` 15 %, of which `PuppetUniformSource::Evaluate` 10.8 %), `SceneRenderState::update` 7 ms,
`wait` 8-13 ms (GPU 6.8), recording only 2.6 ms. Flat profile: SipHash `DefaultHasher` 7 %, malloc/free/`Arc` refcounts
about 12 %, `capture_resources` 9 %. Roughly 55-60 us of CPU per object per frame.

**More Physics**: scripts 177 ms (JS), then 12 ms capture, 7.8 gbegin, 26 ms wait for 8 ms of GPU. Its tower is up to 14 x 32
boxes (448 layers created by script), so it also pays the per-object graph cost of rank 1. After a JIT the script part
falls to roughly 10 ms but the frame stays near 60 ms (about 16 fps) until rank 1 and 4 are done.
JS engines on the wallpaper's own cannon-es (449-body tower, SAP, 8 iterations; `jit/cannon-bench.py`):

| engine | ms/step (300 steps) | first 30 steps | last 30 steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| node 22 (V8 JIT) | 3.05 | 16.3 | 1.33 |
| node --jitless (V8 interpreter) | 35.4 | 190 | 14.0 |
| QuickJS-ng 0.14 -O3 (the engine's revision) | 51.7 | 286 | 20.6 |

So V8's JIT is 17x QuickJS-ng; V8's own interpreter is only 1.5x faster than QuickJS-ng, which bounds what interpreter
tuning (PGO/LTO, fewer allocations) can give. The hypothesis "the JS runtime is the main cost of More Physics" holds
(74 % of its frame, 66.6 % of samples in the interpreter loop); "a JIT alone fixes it" does not: the rest is 60 ms.

**Rain / winter** (55 and 52 particle layers): GPU 3.5 and 2.7 ms, of which 2.2 ms is 46-47 copy passes of the
full frame.

## Not measured (gaps for the next steps)

- Video and web wallpapers: SceneViewer draws scenes only. A WebViewer (CEF) binary exists in other worktrees
  (`owe-av`); video goes through the Waywallen video plugin. Needs their own harness.
- The windowed/presented path and Waywallen's renderer (present, compositor, swapchain pacing), multi-monitor, and
  cold-cache shader compilation (first-frame hitches; the shader cache was warm).
- Per-draw GPU cost inside a pass (needs RenderDoc: not in apt; tarball from renderdoc.org if wanted) and heap
  profiles (needs `sudo apt install heaptrack`). Audio-reactive FFT cost is inside the scripts/prepare stages only.
- Repeat runs: the sweeps are single runs. The regression check tolerates 10 % (and 0.3 ms or 20 MB); take the median of
  three before reading a smaller change.
